Evaluator log - Sponsor Me, Slayers!
=======================================================

=== demo-1-tone : Tone and voice ===
Attempt 1, score 3/10
The checker found no mechanical violations, and the EFFECT line is genuinely correct: it is flat, factual, agrees with the true behaviour on both the 25% instant heal and the Death Save answer, and uses canon terms. Everything wrong here is in the PLUG, and it is wrong in the single way the guide calls an automatic failure. "Someone out there saw you struggling and sent warmth down on a parachute" breaks Type 2 rule 4 outright: it is sincere, heartfelt, warm and encouraging, and it is written to comfort the contestant. That also takes down rule 1, because it is not advertising copy at all and never touches the debt, the odds, or the fact that nobody has finished a run alive, and rule 2, because there is no mockery or contempt anywhere in it. The Network is not selling to someone it expects to die; it sounds like a friend sending a care package. With no sales pitch present, rule 7's joke never fires either, since there is no gap between the pitch and the plain fact for the EFFECT line to land against. On the recognition test, the PLUG in isolation could belong to any game with parachutes and could pass for a hospital charity advert; only the EFFECT line's Death Save tells a stranger which game this is, and the card should not be carrying that weight on one line. Rewrite the PLUG as broadcast copy that sells the turkey leg while being cheerfully cruel about why the contestant needs it: keep the polished corporate register, no slang, and have it point at the debt, the odds, or the corpse count. Something in the register of a sponsor thanking the contestant for the ratings while noting the meal is the most the Network will ever pay out. Leave the EFFECT line exactly as it is.

Attempt 2, score 9/10
No rules were broken. Vocabulary is clean: Sponsor Aid, the Network, and Death Save are all canon, no banned generic or slang words appear, the slot is one of the four legal values, both lines sit under 90 characters, and there are no exclamation marks, em dashes, caps or markdown. The EFFECT line agrees with the true behaviour on both halves, the 25% of maximum health and the contact trigger, and it correctly names Sponsor Aid as the item that resolves the Death Save. A stranger would place this card immediately, since "the Network," "Death Save" and "the only payout it has ever issued" carry the game show and the debt in one breath. Tone is right too: the PLUG is cruel at the contestant's expense, it lands the never-pays-out lore as a joke rather than a threat, and the flat EFFECT line underneath gives you the gap the guide asks for. Two small things keep it off a perfect score. The PLUG reads dry and wry rather than polished and upbeat, so it sounds more like an aside than broadcast advertising copy, and a cheerier framing such as congratulating the contestant on qualifying for their winnings would sell it harder. The EFFECT line's "answers the Death Save" is looser than the rest of the sentence, so tightening it to name the 3-second countdown would make the mechanical half as exact as the 25% half.

=== demo-2-vocabulary : Game vocabulary and lore accuracy ===
Attempt 1, score 2/10
Four mechanical breaks were confirmed by the checker and all four stand. FORMAT: the card is 5 lines, not the required 4; the flag about the timer conflict is a legitimate thing to raise, but it belongs in a separate note outside the deliverable, so delete that line from the card itself. VOCABULARY: "magic" is banned and must go; there is no magic in this game, the Sponsor Aegis is Network hardware. VOCABULARY: "heroes" is banned; the correct word is contestant, singular, because the card is addressed to one debtor on television. VOCABULARY: "monster" is banned; the canon term is hostile, or name the specific type such as Cyber-Swarmer.

On the things the checker cannot judge, the PLUG fails the voice rules outright. "A magic barrier for heroes who would rather charge the horde than dodge it" is heroic register, which Type 2 rule 5 forbids, and it is quietly flattering, which rule 4 forbids: it tells the contestant they are brave. Nothing in it is mocking, nothing mentions the debt, the odds, or the fact that nobody has ever finished a run alive, so it fails rules 1 and 2 as well. It reads as generic fantasy gear copy, and a stranger reading this card could not tell which game it belongs to, which is the exact failure the banned-word list exists to prevent. Rewrite the PLUG as Network advertising: polished, upbeat, and cheerfully certain the contestant will die anyway, for example selling three absorbed hits as a generous investment in someone whose balance the Network expects to write off.

The EFFECT line is the one part that mostly works. Choosing the true behaviour over the "short duration" instruction was correct, and "with no time limit" is the right call. It is flat and factual, and the hit count of 3 is accurate. It only needs "monster" swapped for "hostile" to read: Absorbs the damage of the next 3 hostile hits, with no time limit. Nothing in the card breaks the punctuation, exclamation mark, or ALL CAPS limits.

Rewrite it as a full, cheerful sentence that sells rather than reports, and leave the hit count to the EFFECT line, for example: "Step into the pink and ram the crowd, courtesy of a sponsor betting against you."

Third, Constraint 1.1 in spirit: "the crowd" is the vaguest phrase on the card where a canon hostile name was available. Naming the Cyber-Swarmer or the Cyber-Boar would sharpen the pitch and tie it to the approved list rather than to a generic mass. Fix those three and this is a 10.

=== demo-3-format : Format and length ===
Attempt 1, score 1/10
The card fails nearly every rule in the guide. Format is broken outright: the checker confirms the card has 3 lines where exactly 4 are required, and the required ITEM, SLOT, PLUG and EFFECT labels are all missing or mislabelled, so none of the four lines exist in the shape the guide demands. It uses 11 exclamation marks against a ceiling of 1. It shouts the word "DEMOLITION" in capitals, which the format forbids inside PLUG or EFFECT text, and it also uses markdown bold, which is banned. Length is out by an enormous margin: PLUG and EFFECT are capped at 90 characters each and one sentence each, while this is three long paragraphs of announcer patter running to thousands of characters.

Beyond the mechanical failures, the voice is wrong for the format even though the flavour is close. This reads as a live announcer bark, not as a Network pickup card, and the guide's central joke, the flat factual EFFECT line sitting under the polished sales pitch, is destroyed because the sales copy and the mechanical facts are blended together throughout. The item behaviour is actually stated correctly, six rockets, no resupply anywhere, runs dry inside one wave, drops back to the Standard Pulse Blaster, but it is buried in prose instead of isolated in a flat one-sentence EFFECT line, so the guide's Type 2 rule 7 is still broken. The cruelty is mostly on-target and mocking rather than menacing, and "spend them like a headliner" is good Network contempt, but "go out loud" and "six chances to make the crowd stand up" tip into the heroic, epic register the guide bans, and "make it count" edges towards encouragement.

Lore accuracy is mixed. Rocket Launcher, Prime Time, Standard Pulse Blaster, contestant and the Network are all correct canon terms. However "chassis-mod" is an invented shorthand where the approved list requires an exact hostile name such as Cyber-Swarmer or Cyber-Boar, and "hostiles" is used as a loose generic where a named hostile belongs. A stranger could tell this is a game show shooter, which is the one thing working in its favour.

To fix it: throw out the three paragraphs and write four labelled lines. ITEM: Rocket Launcher. SLOT: Weapon. PLUG: one sentence, 90 characters or fewer, polished Network sales copy that is cheerfully cruel about the debt or the odds, no capitals, at most one exclamation mark on the whole card. EFFECT: one sentence, 90 characters or fewer, flat and factual, stating six rockets, no resupply, and the drop back to the Standard Pulse Blaster. Replace "chassis-mod" with a named hostile or cut the reference, and strip the heroic phrasing entirely.
