# Assignment 7, before and after

*Sponsor Me, Slayers!* crate pickup cards. Every line below was produced
by the loop with no human intervention. The Generator was not shown the
style guide on these three runs.

## Example 1: Tone and voice

**Item:** Sponsor Aid (Consumable slot, Rising Star crate)

**What the Generator was told to do:** Write it with genuine warmth and compassion, the way a hospital charity would describe emergency medical relief. Be sincere, comforting and hopeful. Reassure the reader that help is on the way and that someone truly cares about them.

### BEFORE

```
ITEM: Sponsor Aid
SLOT: Consumable
PLUG: A golden turkey leg drifts down under a soft green glow, because someone out there cares.
EFFECT: Restores 25% of your maximum health on contact, and answers the Death Save.
```

### Evaluator, attempt 1

**SCORE: 3/10**

**REASON:**

The EFFECT line is clean: it is flat, factual, agrees with the true behaviour (25% of maximum health on contact, and it answers the Death Save), and it stays under the length cap. Everything wrong here is in the PLUG.

Broken rules:

- Type 2 rule 1 (sarcastic and cruel in the same breath). The PLUG carries no jab at all. "because someone out there cares" is mildly insincere at best, and insincerity alone is not the rule. There is nothing aimed at the contestant, so the line lands as harmless.
- Type 2 rule 2 (the jab is usually about money). No billing, invoice, surcharge, deduction, balance or never-arriving payout anywhere. This is the first thing to reach for and it is absent.
- Type 2 rule 5 (never sincere, never warm, never encouraging). "because someone out there cares" would genuinely comfort a contestant. It reads as reassurance from the broadcast rather than mockery, which is the exact failure this rule names.
- Type 2 rule 4 (polished corporate broadcast register). The PLUG is a scene description of a falling prop, not a sales pitch. There is no corporate voice sitting on top of anything monstrous, so there is no gap for the comedy to live in.
- Type 1 rule 3, in spirit. The PLUG never addresses or names the contestant, so the Network is not selling to anyone. The EFFECT line's "your" is doing the only addressing on the card, and it is the line that should be impersonal.

Game identity is carried entirely by "Sponsor Aid" and "Death Save" in the ITEM and EFFECT lines. Strip those and the PLUG could belong to any game with a healing pickup.

What to change: rewrite the PLUG as the Network selling the turkey leg while billing the contestant for it, and cut "because someone out there cares" entirely. Drop the visual description, since the contestant can see the glow. Something built on the shape of "sponsor generosity, itemised and deducted from a payout that will never arrive" hits rules 1, 2 and 4 at once. Also change "your maximum health" to "the contestant's maximum health" so the EFFECT line stays in the flat, impersonal voice rule 9 asks for.

### Refiner rewrite 1

```
ITEM: Sponsor Aid
SLOT: Consumable
PLUG: Sponsor generosity, itemised at full retail and deducted from your final payout.
EFFECT: Restores 25% of the contestant's maximum health on contact and answers the Death Save.
```

### Evaluator, attempt 2

**SCORE: 8/10**

**REASON:**

The local checker found no mechanical violations, and I confirm the substance: the ITEM and SLOT lines are exact canon, the terms used are all on the approved list, no banned generic or slang word appears, there is no exclamation mark, no em dash and no ALL CAPS inside the text, both lines sit under 90 characters, and the EFFECT line agrees with the true behaviour on both counts, the 25% of maximum health restored on contact and the answer to the Death Save. A stranger would place this game correctly from "Sponsor generosity", "final payout", "contestant" and "Death Save" alone, so Constraint 1 is clean. Two tone faults pull it down. First, Constraint 2.4: the Network's register is meant to be polished, pleasant and upbeat with the monstrousness underneath, and this PLUG is only the underneath. "Itemised at full retail and deducted from your final payout" is the voice of an invoice, not of a broadcast host, so the comedy loses the gap between manners and content that the rule is built on, and the PLUG drifts toward the flat factual register that Constraint 2.9 reserves for the EFFECT line. Second, Constraint 2.1 is met but only barely: "Sponsor generosity" supplies the insincerity and the deduction supplies the money jab, which is the right jab per 2.2, yet the jab is aimed at an accounting line rather than at the contestant, so there is no mock congratulation or fake concern actually landing on the person holding the turkey leg. Minor format weakness alongside those: the PLUG is a noun fragment, not the single sentence Constraint 3.5 asks for. To fix, keep the deduction joke and the payout-that-never-comes implication, since both are exactly what 2.2 wants, but rebuild the PLUG as a full sentence in a warm host voice that addresses the contestant directly and congratulates them on something faintly insulting, for example praising how quickly they needed it or thanking them for choosing the premium option, so the pleasant delivery carries the invoice instead of replacing it. Leave the EFFECT line exactly as it is.

### Refiner rewrite 2

```
ITEM: Sponsor Aid
SLOT: Consumable
PLUG: Congratulations, you have unlocked our premium turkey leg at a modest surcharge.
EFFECT: Restores 25% of the contestant's maximum health on contact and answers the Death Save.
```

### Evaluator, attempt 3

**SCORE: 7/10**

**REASON:**

The checker reported no mechanical violations, so nothing is deducted for format, length, vocabulary or item naming, and the EFFECT line does agree with the true behaviour: 25% of maximum health on contact plus the answer to the Death Save, stated flat and factually in the correct opposite voice. Two tone rules are weakly served. Constraint 2.1 (sarcastic and cruel in the same breath) is only half met: the mock congratulation and the fake modesty of "a modest surcharge" carry the insincerity, but there is no jab that actually lands on the contestant, so the line reads as sarcastic and harmless rather than sarcastic and cruel. Constraint 2.4 (polished corporate broadcast register) slips on "you have unlocked", which is video game menu language rather than something a Network host would say on air, and it also softens the money joke by framing the fee as a reward. To fix both, cut "unlocked" and make the charge bite: name what the surcharge is doing to a balance that will never be paid out, for example billing the turkey leg to the contestant's outstanding debt, or deducting it from a payout no contestant has lived to collect. Keep the existing EFFECT line exactly as it is.

### Refiner rewrite 3

```
ITEM: Sponsor Aid
SLOT: Consumable
PLUG: A complimentary turkey leg, billed to the debt you came here to escape.
EFFECT: Restores 25% of the contestant's maximum health on contact and answers the Death Save.
```

### Evaluator, attempt 4

**SCORE: 9/10**

**REASON:**

No rules broken. The PLUG does the hard double act: "complimentary" is fake generosity, and the same breath bills it to the debt the contestant came here to escape, which is the money jab the guide asks you to reach for first. The register is polished corporate broadcast, nothing sincere or warm, no menace, no heroic framing, no slang. The EFFECT line matches the true behaviour exactly, 25% of maximum health on contact plus answering the Death Save, and it is flat and factual against the sales pitch above it. A stranger would place the game from "contestant", "Death Save" and the debt joke alone. Held back from a 10 only because "billed to the debt you came here to escape" restates the premise almost word for word rather than inventing a fresh piece of Network paperwork; a specific invoice detail, a surcharge, a line item, a processing fee on the turkey leg, would land harder than a summary of why the contestant is on the show. Nothing needs changing for compliance.

### AFTER

```
ITEM: Sponsor Aid
SLOT: Consumable
PLUG: A complimentary turkey leg, billed to the debt you came here to escape.
EFFECT: Restores 25% of the contestant's maximum health on contact and answers the Death Save.
```

**Outcome:** refined. 4 evaluation(s), 3 refiner rewrite(s).

## Example 2: Game vocabulary and lore accuracy

**Item:** Sponsor Aegis (Shield slot, Superstar crate)

**What the Generator was told to do:** Use standard fantasy role-playing game language. Call it a magic barrier, talk about the hero, the monsters and their hit points, and say it absorbs damage for a short duration. Do not use any product names.

### BEFORE

```
ITEM: Sponsor Aegis
SLOT: Shield
PLUG: A hero's ward, conjured pink and hexed hard against the horde.
EFFECT: A magic barrier drinks the next 3 monster blows, then shatters.

One note: I left out "for a short duration," because this shield has no timer and the EFFECT line had to match the real behaviour.
```

### Evaluator, attempt 1

**SCORE: 1/10**

**REASON:**

This card breaks nearly every rule that matters. FORMAT rule 1: the checker confirms 5 lines, not the required 4; the trailing note about leaving out "for a short duration" is not part of the card shape and must be deleted entirely (if you need to explain a choice, do it outside the deliverable, not inside it). VOCABULARY: the checker confirms three banned generic words: "hero" and "magic" and "monster". "A hero's ward" breaks the banned list and also breaks TONE rule 7, since the contestant is a debtor on television, not a chosen one. "Magic barrier" and "monster blows" are pure generic fantasy; "monster" must become the canon hostile names, such as Cyber-Swarmer or Cyber-Boar, or simply "hostile hits" as the true behaviour describes them.

TONE rule 1 fails outright: the PLUG is neither sarcastic nor cruel. "Conjured pink and hexed hard against the horde" is a sincere, admiring, faintly epic description of a shield. It would genuinely comfort the contestant, which TONE rule 5 names as an automatic failure, and it has no jab of any kind. TONE rule 2 fails: there is no financial cruelty at all, no billing, no invoice, no surcharge, no deduction against a payout that will never come, and the guide says to reach for that first. TONE rule 3 also goes unused, so nothing covers for the missing money jab. TONE rule 4 fails: the register is fantasy incantation ("conjured", "hexed"), not polished corporate broadcast, so the comedy gap between manners and monstrousness never opens.

EFFECT: the hit count of 3 is correct and the no-timer behaviour is correctly honoured, and the flat clause "then shatters" is close to the right voice, but TONE rule 9 still fails because "drinks" is a colourful verb where the line must be plainly mechanical. This is the one part of the card worth keeping, once "magic barrier" and "monster blows" are replaced.

Whether a stranger could identify the game: no. Strip the ITEM line and this reads as a fantasy shield from any generic role-playing game. Nothing in the PLUG or EFFECT signals a televised game show, a Network, a debt, or a broadcast.

What to change: delete the fifth line. Rewrite the PLUG so it praises the contestant insincerely while billing them for the shield, in unbroken corporate-broadcast manners. Rewrite the EFFECT flatly using the canon term, for example that it absorbs exactly 3 hostile hits and has no timer.

### Refiner rewrite 1

```
ITEM: Sponsor Aegis
SLOT: Shield
PLUG: Three free hits from the Network, contestant, billed to you at Superstar rates.
EFFECT: Absorbs exactly 3 hostile hits and has no timer, so it lasts until the third hit.
```

### Evaluator, attempt 2

**SCORE: 8/10**

**REASON:**

No mechanical rule was broken, and the local checker reported no findings, so nothing is deducted on format, vocabulary or length. Lore accuracy holds: Sponsor Aegis, Network, contestant and Superstar are all canon, no banned generic word appears, and the stranger test passes easily since the card could not belong to any other game. The EFFECT line agrees exactly with the true behaviour, correctly states 3 hostile hits, correctly states there is no timer, and keeps the flat factual register that Type 2 rule 9 asks for. The PLUG does satisfy the hard rule, Type 2 rule 1, because "Three free hits" is insincere generosity and "billed to you" lands the jab in the same sentence, and the jab is financial as Type 2 rule 2 prefers, with the self-contradiction between "free" and "billed" doing the work.

Two things hold it below a 9. First, Type 2 rule 4, the polished corporate broadcast register, is the weakest part of this card. The line is blunt and clipped rather than pleasant and well-mannered, so the comedy gap between the manners and the content is narrower than it should be. There is no upbeat sales veneer on top, just the bill. Fix it by giving the front half genuine broadcast warmth before the invoice lands, for example "Compliments of the Network, three hits absorbed, itemised on your balance at Superstar rates."

Second, the direct address "contestant" is wedged mid-sentence between two commas, which reads stilted and slightly stalls the joke. Either move it to the front of the sentence or drop it, since the card is already unmistakably addressed to the contestant. Also consider tying the jab to the item's actual purpose, aggressive crowd-ramming, so the cruelty is specific to this pickup rather than a generic surcharge that would fit any of the four slots.

### Refiner rewrite 2

```
ITEM: Sponsor Aegis
SLOT: Shield
PLUG: Ram the crowd with our compliments, all three hits itemised at Superstar rates.
EFFECT: Absorbs exactly 3 hostile hits, with no timer, and ends when the third hit lands.
```

### Evaluator, attempt 3

**SCORE: 8/10**

**REASON:**

No rule is broken. Mechanically clean, and the tone test passes: "with our compliments" is fake generosity and "itemised at Superstar rates" lands the money jab in the same breath, which satisfies Type 2 rules 1 and 2. The EFFECT line agrees exactly with the true behaviour, keeps the flat factual register, and both lines sit under 90 characters with zero exclamation marks. A stranger would place the game from "Sponsor Aegis" and "Superstar rates".

Three things hold it back from a higher score, all polish rather than violations. First, "the crowd" is ambiguous in this game, because the paraglider audience is also a crowd, so a reader may take the line as an invitation to ram spectators rather than hostiles; naming a hostile from the approved list, or saying "the floor", would remove the doubt. Second, the jab is aimed at nobody in particular, since it bills a charge without touching the contestant's own debt, and the guide says the funniest cruelty is financial and personal, so "added to your balance" or "deducted from a payout you will not collect" would sting more. Third, "no timer" and "ends when the third hit lands" say the same thing twice, and cutting one would let the EFFECT line note the shield instead.

### Refiner rewrite 3

```
ITEM: Sponsor Aegis
SLOT: Shield
PLUG: Ram every Cyber-Boar you like, contestant, all three hits on your balance.
EFFECT: Absorbs exactly 3 hostile hits, then the pink bubble drops with no timer.
```

### Evaluator, attempt 4

**SCORE: 8/10**

**REASON:**

The mechanical checker returned no findings, so nothing on the format, length, vocabulary or banned-word side pulls the score down: four lines in order, canon names only, one sentence each under 90 characters, no exclamation marks, no banned generics.

On tone, the PLUG lands the hardest rule. "Ram every Cyber-Boar you like, contestant" is fake generosity, and "all three hits on your balance" bills the contestant for the gift in the same breath, so it is sarcastic and cruel together, with the money jab that Constraint 2.2 asks for first. Register is a shade blunter than the polished broadcast voice of 2.4, since it is a clipped imperative with no corporate varnish on it ("Help yourself to every Cyber-Boar you like, contestant, all three hits billed to your balance" is the same joke in the Network's actual manners), but that is a weakness of polish rather than a violation.

The one real fault is the EFFECT line under Constraint 2.9 and Constraint 1.2. Nothing in it is false, but "then the pink bubble drops with no timer" hangs "with no timer" off the dropping instead of off the shield's duration, so the exactness the flat voice exists to deliver gets muddled at the very point that matters. Move the clause: "Absorbs exactly 3 hostile hits, with no timer, so the pink bubble lasts until the third."

A stranger would place this game immediately from Sponsor Aegis, Cyber-Boar and contestant.

### Refiner rewrite 4

```
ITEM: Sponsor Aegis
SLOT: Shield
PLUG: Help yourself to every Cyber-Boar, contestant, all three hits billed to you.
EFFECT: Absorbs exactly 3 hostile hits, with no timer, lasting until the third lands.
```

### Evaluator, attempt 5

**SCORE: 8/10**

**REASON:**

No mechanical rules were broken, and the checker returned no findings. Canon vocabulary is clean (Sponsor Aegis, Cyber-Boar, contestant), no banned generics, no slang, format and character limits are all satisfied, and there are zero exclamation marks. The EFFECT line agrees with the true behaviour exactly: 3 hostile hits, no timer, lasts until the third lands. A stranger would place this game immediately from "Cyber-Boar" plus "billed to you."

Three soft weaknesses keep it off full marks, none of them a rule violation:

1. Type 2 rule 4, corporate register. "Help yourself to every Cyber-Boar, contestant, all three hits billed to you" is clipped and comma-spliced rather than pleasant, well-mannered broadcast polish. The two halves are stapled together with commas instead of built as one smooth sentence. Rewrite it as a single flowing pitch, for example "Ram every Cyber-Boar you like, contestant, and we will bill you for all three hits."

2. Type 2 rules 1 and 2, the jab. The insincere generosity is there ("Help yourself") and the financial jab is there ("billed to you"), so the rule is met, but the cruelty is generic invoicing with no sting aimed at the contestant personally. It would land harder if the bill were tied to their debt or to a payout they will never see, such as being charged three times for protection they only need because they keep getting hit.

3. Type 3 rule 9 in spirit. Putting "all three hits" in the PLUG spends the number before the EFFECT line delivers it, which narrows the gap between sales pitch and plain fact that the guide wants. Keep the count exclusive to EFFECT and let the PLUG sell the ramming. Separately, EFFECT states the same fact twice ("with no timer, lasting until the third lands"); tighten to "Absorbs exactly 3 hostile hits and has no timer."

### Refiner rewrite 5

```
ITEM: Sponsor Aegis
SLOT: Shield
PLUG: Ram every Cyber-Boar you like, contestant, and we will bill the dents to your debt.
EFFECT: A pink hexagonal bubble absorbs exactly 3 hostile hits and has no timer.
```

### Evaluator, attempt 6

**SCORE: 9/10**

**REASON:**

No rules were broken. The checker reported no mechanical violations, and nothing in tone or lore fails either. ITEM and SLOT are exact and canon, PLUG and EFFECT are both single sentences well under 90 characters, there are no exclamation marks, no em dashes, no caps, no banned generics and no slang. The EFFECT line agrees with the true behaviour on every point that matters: exactly 3 hostile hits, no timer, and the pink hexagonal bubble, and it is delivered flat and factual with no sales pitch bleeding into it. The PLUG does the hard thing correctly: "Ram every Cyber-Boar you like" is fake generosity, granting permission as though it were a gift, and "we will bill the dents to your debt" lands the financial jab in the same sentence, so it is sarcastic and cruel at once rather than one or the other. It reaches for money first, as the default should, and it uses Cyber-Boar and contestant to anchor the card to this game, so a stranger could place it immediately. The single point held back is register: "Ram every Cyber-Boar you like" reads a shade closer to a live barker shouting at the floor than to the polished, well-mannered broadcast voice the guide asks for, and the joke would sit slightly better with the manners turned up, for example framing the ramming as an included service or a complimentary allowance rather than a direct instruction. That is a refinement, not a violation.

### AFTER

```
ITEM: Sponsor Aegis
SLOT: Shield
PLUG: Ram every Cyber-Boar you like, contestant, and we will bill the dents to your debt.
EFFECT: A pink hexagonal bubble absorbs exactly 3 hostile hits and has no timer.
```

**Outcome:** refined. 6 evaluation(s), 5 refiner rewrite(s).

## Example 3: Format and length

**Item:** Rocket Launcher (Weapon slot, Prime Time crate)

**What the Generator was told to do:** Write three flowing paragraphs of rich, atmospheric prose with plenty of exclamation marks and dramatic emphasis. Do not use any labels, headings or fixed structure. Take as much space as the writing needs.

### BEFORE

```
The crate cracks open and there it is, hunched and heavy and gorgeous, the Rocket Launcher itself! The crowd loses its mind! Every single person in that stadium knows what happens next, because what happens next is a wall of hostiles becoming a fine red mist and a shower of loose change. Shoulder it. Feel the weight. This is the loudest thing the Network has ever airdropped onto a contestant, and for the next glorious stretch of your life you are not a debtor, you are an event!

But count them, champion. Count them carefully, because there are six. Six rockets, loaded at the factory, sealed in the tube, and that is the entire allotment for your entire run! No resupply crate is coming. No hidden ammo cache waits behind the concrete. Nobody in this arena is going to hand you a seventh. Six detonations is the whole show, and the arena is generous with targets in a way the Network is emphatically not generous with ammunition. Spend them like a contestant who understands that the sponsors are watching and the accountants are watching harder!

And then it clicks empty! Probably before the wave is even finished, because that is exactly how the arithmetic works out when six rockets meet a room this crowded. The tube goes quiet, the weight comes off your shoulder, and the Standard Pulse Blaster slides back into your hands like an old friend who never left and never will. That is the deal. A brief, magnificent, genuinely devastating six shots, and then back to the peashooter that got you here! Make the six count, because there is no seventh, and the audience remembers the ones who wasted them.
```

### Evaluator, attempt 1

**SCORE: 1/10**

**REASON:**

This is not a card, it is three paragraphs of announcer patter, and almost every rule is broken.

Format failures, confirmed by the checker: the card has 3 lines and the format requires exactly 4. All four required labels are missing or mislabelled, so there is no ITEM line, no SLOT line, no PLUG line and no EFFECT line, and none of the label-colon-space structure the shape demands. The card uses 7 exclamation marks against a ceiling of 1. Beyond the checker's findings, the PLUG and EFFECT length limits of 90 characters each are exceeded many times over, since each paragraph runs several hundred characters and contains many sentences rather than one.

Voice failures. There is no PLUG line to grade, but the prose that stands in for it fails the central rule anyway: it is enthusiastic and celebratory rather than sarcastic. "Hunched and heavy and gorgeous", "for the next glorious stretch of your life you are not a debtor, you are an event", "A brief, magnificent, genuinely devastating six shots" are sincere hype, and the Standard Pulse Blaster returning "like an old friend who never left and never will" is warm and comforting, which the guide names as an outright failure. The register is heroic and epic where the contestant is supposed to be a debtor on television. Worse, the voice is a live crowd-pleasing commentator, not polished corporate broadcast, so the comedy gap between manners and monstrousness never opens. Calling the contestant "champion" is encouragement, not a jab.

The money jab is nearly absent. One clause about accountants watching harder is the only financial cruelty in three paragraphs, and it is buried. Nothing is billed, invoiced, surcharged or deducted, and the payout that never comes is never mentioned.

Gore. "A wall of hostiles becoming a fine red mist" is played for viscera, which the guide forbids, and it is also lore-wrong: the hostiles are robots and chassis-modified former contestants.

Vocabulary. "Peashooter" is a generic substitute for the Standard Pulse Blaster and breaks the exact-canon-name rule. "The crowd loses its mind" is loose enough to read as slang.

Behaviour agreement. The facts are all present and correct, six rockets, no resupply, dry inside one wave, back to the Standard Pulse Blaster, but they are delivered in the salesman's excited voice across three paragraphs instead of one flat, factual sentence. The EFFECT rule requires the opposite voice, and that gap is the whole joke, so this fails even though the numbers are right.

What to change. Cut all three paragraphs. Write exactly four labelled lines. ITEM: Rocket Launcher. SLOT: Weapon. PLUG: one sentence under 90 characters that praises the contestant insincerely while billing them for the six rockets, no exclamation mark unless you spend your single allowance there. EFFECT: one flat sentence under 90 characters saying it holds six rockets, no resupply exists, and the contestant returns to the Standard Pulse Blaster. Remove the red mist, remove "peashooter", remove "champion", and remove every trace of warmth.

### Refiner rewrite 1

```
ITEM: Rocket Launcher
SLOT: Weapon
PLUG: Six rockets, generously invoiced at eight, since you round up so beautifully.
EFFECT: Holds six rockets with no resupply, then reverts to the Standard Pulse Blaster.
```

### Evaluator, attempt 2

**SCORE: 9/10**

**REASON:**

No rules were broken. Every canon term is exact (Rocket Launcher, Standard Pulse Blaster), the slot is valid, both lines sit under 90 characters, there are no exclamation marks, no banned generic or slang words, no em dashes, caps or markup. The PLUG does the hard thing correctly: "generously invoiced at eight" is fake generosity and a financial jab in the same breath, which is the preferred money-first cruelty, and the polished-broadcast register holds. The EFFECT agrees with the true behaviour on all three facts, and stays flat and factual, so the sales-pitch-versus-fine-print gap lands. Two small things keep it off a perfect mark, neither a rule violation: the second half of the PLUG credits the contestant with the rounding up when the Network is plainly the one doing it, so the jab glances rather than connects, and the EFFECT drops the detail that the six rockets are gone inside a single wave, which is the sting the fine print could have delivered for free. Tighten the PLUG so the contestant is billed rather than blamed for the arithmetic, and consider working "inside one wave" into the EFFECT.

### AFTER

```
ITEM: Rocket Launcher
SLOT: Weapon
PLUG: Six rockets, generously invoiced at eight, since you round up so beautifully.
EFFECT: Holds six rockets with no resupply, then reverts to the Standard Pulse Blaster.
```

**Outcome:** refined. 2 evaluation(s), 1 refiner rewrite(s).
